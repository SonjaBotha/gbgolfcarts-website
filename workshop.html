Should You Switch to Lithium Batteries?

By Willie · From the Workshop

Everyone is asking me about lithium batteries at the moment.

And I understand why. They are lighter, they last longer, and you don't have to check water levels or clean acid off the terminals.

But they are also expensive. So let me tell you honestly when it makes sense — and when it doesn't.

What I like about lithium

  • Lighter: Your cart feels completely different. Less weight means it pulls better, especially uphill.
  • Longer life: A good lithium set can last 8 to 10 years if you look after it. Lead-acid usually gives you 3 to 4 years.
  • No maintenance: No water, no acid, no cleaning terminals every month.
  • More consistent power: It doesn't go flat halfway up the hill like an old lead-acid set does.

What you must know before you switch

This is the part most adverts don't tell you:

  • It costs more upfront. Yes, you save in the long run, but you must be ready for that first bill.
  • You need the right charger. You cannot just put a lithium battery on your old lead-acid charger. That is how people damage a brand new set in the first month.
  • Not all lithium is the same. I have seen cheap imports with no proper BMS — that's the brain that protects the battery. Without it, you have problems.
  • Your cart must be checked first. If your wiring, controller or motor is already struggling, a new battery won't fix it. It will just hide the problem for a while.

So, should you do it?

Here is my honest rule:

If you use your cart almost every day, and you plan to keep it for 5 years or more — yes, lithium is worth it.

If you only use your cart once a month for a round of golf, and your lead-acid batteries are still good — keep them. Don't spend money you don't need to spend.

"Don't buy a battery because of an advert. Bring your cart in, let me test what you have, and then we decide together what makes sense for you."

— Willie

If you are thinking about switching, bring your cart in. I will test your old batteries, check your charger, and tell you straight if lithium is right for you — or if you are better off waiting.
